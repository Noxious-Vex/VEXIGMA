VEXIGMA Security Policy

VEXIGMA takes good-faith security research seriously.

This document explains how to report suspected security vulnerabilities
affecting VEXIGMA --- Advanced Cipher Laboratory and describes the
project's coordinated vulnerability-disclosure process.

Supported Versions

Until a more detailed support schedule is published, security reports
should focus on the latest Official Release of VEXIGMA.

Older releases may no longer receive security fixes, updates, or
support. NoxiousVex does not guarantee maintenance or continued
availability of any VEXIGMA version.

Reporting a Vulnerability

Please do not publicly disclose an unpatched vulnerability as the
first method of reporting it.

Send security reports privately to:

NoxiousVex
Email: NoxiousVex1993@gmail.com

If the official VEXIGMA GitHub repository later enables GitHub's private
vulnerability-reporting or Security Advisory features, those official
private channels may also be used.

Do not include sensitive third-party personal information, credentials,
private keys, or unrelated confidential data unless it is genuinely
necessary to explain the vulnerability.

What to Include

A useful vulnerability report should include, where reasonably
available:

the affected VEXIGMA version;

the operating environment;

a clear description of the suspected vulnerability;

steps needed to reproduce it;

expected and observed behaviour;

the potential security impact;

relevant screenshots, logs, proof-of-concept material, or test
files;

whether the issue has been disclosed to anyone else; and

a safe method of contacting the reporter if follow-up is needed.

A report does not need to be perfect. Clear information sufficient to
understand and reproduce the issue is more important than a particular
format.

Coordinated Disclosure Period

VEXIGMA uses a coordinated disclosure period of up to ninety (90)
days.

The period begins when NoxiousVex receives a sufficiently detailed
private vulnerability report that reasonably identifies the issue.

During this period, a good-faith researcher relying on the
security-research permissions granted by the VEXIGMA License is expected
to provide NoxiousVex a reasonable opportunity to investigate and
remediate the issue before publishing technical details.

NoxiousVex may authorize disclosure before the 90-day period expires.

The parties may also mutually agree to a different disclosure date when
circumstances reasonably justify it.

Nothing in this policy is intended to prevent a disclosure that
applicable law protects or requires.

What NoxiousVex May Do After a Report

Depending on the report and available resources, NoxiousVex may:

acknowledge receipt;

request additional information;

attempt to reproduce and assess the issue;

develop or test a correction;

release an updated version;

publish a security advisory;

coordinate a disclosure date with the reporter; or

determine that the reported behaviour is not considered a VEXIGMA
security vulnerability.

Submission of a report does not guarantee a response time, fix, update,
bounty, credit, or continued support for an affected version.

Good-Faith Security Research

The VEXIGMA License permits defined good-faith security research and
interoperability testing subject to its terms and applicable law.

Research should be conducted in a manner intended to avoid harm.

Researchers should not:

intentionally access data, accounts, systems, or devices they are
not authorized to access;

unnecessarily collect, retain, modify, destroy, or disclose
third-party data;

deploy malware or intentionally harm users or systems;

use a vulnerability for fraud, extortion, harassment, or other
malicious activity;

intentionally degrade or disrupt systems beyond what is reasonably
necessary for authorized testing;

use research as a means to bypass VEXIGMA licensing for unauthorized
purposes;

use discovered information to distribute an unauthorized modified or
competing VEXIGMA build; or

publicly disclose technical vulnerability details contrary to the
coordinated-disclosure requirements when relying on permissions
granted by the VEXIGMA License.

Researchers remain responsible for complying with applicable law and for
obtaining authorization for systems, accounts, networks, devices, and
data they test.

Security Research Is Not a License to Attack Third Parties

Permission to research VEXIGMA does not authorize testing against
third-party systems or users without their permission.

Where testing can be performed against a locally controlled copy, test
environment, or other system the researcher is authorized to use, that
approach should be preferred.

Proof-of-Concept Material

Proof-of-concept material may be included privately when reasonably
necessary to demonstrate or reproduce a vulnerability.

Avoid creating unnecessarily destructive proof-of-concept code or
collecting unrelated data.

Public release of exploit details should follow the
coordinated-disclosure process described above when the researcher
relies on permissions granted by the VEXIGMA License.

Cryptographic Security Notice

VEXIGMA is experimental cryptographic software.

It is not represented or warranted as meeting modern cryptographic
security standards and may not have undergone independent cryptographic
review or security certification.

A weakness in VEXIGMA's cipher design, implementation, key handling,
machine configuration, or related functionality may still be valuable to
report, particularly if the behaviour differs from VEXIGMA's documented
operation or creates an unexpected security risk.

However, the experimental nature of VEXIGMA should be considered when
evaluating claims about its suitability for protecting sensitive
information.

VEXIGMA should not be relied upon to protect sensitive, confidential,
safety-critical, financial, authentication, high-value, or similarly
consequential information.

Security Bounties

VEXIGMA does not currently operate a bug-bounty program.

Submitting a vulnerability report does not create an entitlement to
payment, compensation, employment, royalties, or any other reward unless
NoxiousVex separately agrees to one in writing.

Public Recognition

NoxiousVex may, where appropriate, acknowledge a researcher who
responsibly reports a vulnerability.

Any recognition is discretionary. A researcher may request not to be
publicly identified.
